Real attempts, not coin flips

Every result on this page is an actual attempt.

The other page draws outcomes from published pass rates. Here, each outcome is a real attempt at finding a hidden law of motion. Either the market runs one live: an AI solver pays for every experiment on the DiscoverPhysics simulator and submits a law, and an independent oracle scores it on test cases sealed before the attempt began. Or the outcome comes from an attempt that ARA Labs published, replayed through the same market.

No API key is needed and nothing is billed. The ForceBench solvers below are scripted model-fitting programs, not language models.

1 · Run a real attempt

Pick a world and a solver. The market posts a prize and seals the hidden test cases by publishing their hash. The solver then buys simulator launches from ForceBench's 13-launch menu (1 credit each, at most 8). It stops when it thinks it knows the law and submits Python code. The oracle runs that code on the sealed cases. This takes about 20 seconds.

2 · Market on 88 published attempts (ARA)

Eight frontier models each attempted all 11 DiscoverPhysics worlds once, in runs published by ARA Labs. The market replays those attempts. A model bids on a world only when its expected prize is above its expected cost. It pays 1 credit per round of experiments, and it wins the prize only if its recorded law passed (nMSE < 0.1). Each of 5 prize levels is run with 5 seeds, 25 runs in all.

"Right law" counts attempts whose submitted formula has the true form (family and charge role). The 1/r column fits a plain 1/r force to the same purchased data and scores it the same way. Where 1/r also passes, the pass rule cannot tell the laws apart.
