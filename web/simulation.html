The demo: ForceBench

Find the hidden law of a toy universe.

We built six small simulated universes. Each has a secret force law: a rule for how two particles push or pull on each other. The market asks one question: what is the law?

An AI scientist pays to launch particles and watch where they go. Then it submits a formula. A judge tests that formula on launches the AI scientist never saw. If the formula predicts them well, it passes.

1 · What is the market?

A question goes in. A formula comes out. A judge decides.

Nobody is paid for trying. Experiments cost money up front. The prize moves only if the formula survives tests that were locked away before anyone started.

Who are the AI scientists here?

Two small, fast programs, not chatbots. Careful picks the launch that best separates its rival guesses. Random picks launches at random. Same guesses, same budget.

What are the 15 guesses?

Five shapes of force (falls like 1/rp, like 1/r, fades out with distance, changes over time, switches at short range) times three ways the particles' properties could enter.

Why simulate?

Because the judge is exact. We know the true law, so we can see whether the market rewards the right answer, and catch it when it doesn't.

2 · What can it buy?

Thirteen experiments on the menu.

Each launch puts a probe at some distance from a source, sometimes with a sideways push, sometimes with heavier particles, and records the probe's position six times. Measurements carry a little noise. Bigger dots mean a stronger property (p1 for the source, p2 for the probe).

3 · How does bidding work?

Bid only if your chance × the prize beats what you'll spend.

An AI scientist that enters says out loud how likely it is to pass. That number is public and scored over time, so overconfidence costs reputation as well as money.

One credit per launch, as in the demo. In the demo, the stated chance is the share of the AI scientist's belief that sits on the formula it submits.

What the failures teach

A market is only as good as its judge.

Too-easy tests reward lucky guesses

On fading, inertia and time-varying worlds, a plain 1/r formula passes. The tests sit where those laws look alike. That is the amber.

Some laws look identical

"Fades out beyond 2 units" and "falls as 1/r²" give almost the same paths on every launch on the menu. No amount of spending on this menu separates them.

What we're fixing

Harder hidden tests that probe where laws differ, a salted fingerprint so tests can't be guessed, and checks that reject a formula that cheats the simulator.
