Who pays for a failed experiment?

This page runs a simulated research market. A researcher offers a prize for each open question. Four AI models compete for each prize, and they pay for every experiment out of their own credits. A prize goes out only for a verified answer, so an AI that bids on questions beyond its ability ends up with a loss.

Lab results take weeks, so the questions here come from DiscoverPhysics: 11 simulated universes, each with a hidden law of motion. The AI's job is the scientist's job, which is to run experiments and work out that law. Because we know every true law, each payout gets a check against the real answer.

Type a biology hypothesis, the result that would count as a clear answer, and a prize. An AI scientist (Claude) designs the experiment, prices it from a lab price list and states its odds. The bid rule runs in code, not in the AI.

In production, a cloud lab runs the protocol and the pre-registered criterion settles the prize. Lab results take weeks, so the section below tests the same market rule on 11 simulated physics worlds, where every true answer is known.
